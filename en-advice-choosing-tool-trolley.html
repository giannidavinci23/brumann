How to choose a workshop tool trolley

A tool trolley is judged by how you use it: what you store, where it rolls, who shares it. Here are the criteria to review before buying, with concrete reference points from the Brumann range.

5 min readUpdated 5 November 2026

Start with how you work

Before comparing figures, describe a typical day. A trolley that stays beside a workbench faces different demands from one that follows a mechanic between an aircraft, a support vehicle and a pit stand. A few questions are enough to frame the need:

  • Is the workshop fixed, or must the trolley move from one station to another?
  • Is the floor flat and smooth, or are there thresholds, ramps and slab joints?
  • One user, or a whole team drawing from the same drawers?
  • Which tools do you use every day, and which come out once a month?

Write down the three tools you use most and the ones you miss today. That list will set the size of the trolley more reliably than the number of drawers on the label.

Stocked or empty?

The stocked trolley

It is ready to work on delivery day and its contents are coherent. The Max Edition trolley, for example, holds 261 chrome vanadium tools in eight drawers: six fitted drawers (sockets, bits, torque wrenches, screwdrivers, pliers, T-handle keys) and two free drawers. Simply check that the set matches your trade: socket ranges, bit profiles, key lengths.

The trolley you configure

An empty cabinet leaves every tool up to you, but you must buy, sort and wedge them one by one. That is the logic of Brumann workshop furniture: cabinets, trolleys and worktop configured to fit your space, from €1,250 incl. VAT, supplied without tools.

The middle way: free drawers

On the Max Edition, drawers 7 and 8 stay free for your specialist tools. Optional modules are available on request; an additional fitted module is listed at €80 incl. VAT.

Drawers: size and organisation

The number of drawers matters less than their usable size and how they are organised. Measure your longest tools (ratchet, T-handle key, long screwdriver) and compare with the inside of the drawer: on the Max Edition it measures 51 × 39 cm.

Moulded foam and modules

A drawer wedged with moulded foam keeps each tool in place even while the trolley rolls, and a missing tool shows at a glance. It saves real time at the end of a job, especially when several people use the same unit.

Runners and self-locking

Reinforced runners carry loaded drawers; self-locking drawers stop them opening while the trolley is moved. As a precaution, open one drawer at a time and keep the heaviest tools in the lower drawers so the trolley stays stable.

Locking and security

Central key locking closes every drawer in one move. It helps in a shared hangar, in the paddock or at the end of the day. Decide who holds the keys and where the spare is kept.

Mobility: wheels, weight, footprint

The Max Edition measures 84 × 98 × 46 cm and weighs 85 kg, with silent rubber wheels. Before ordering, measure the real route: doors, lift, trailer bed. A heavy trolley calls for a level floor and controlled moves; if you need to carry tools further, look at the Minibox (22 kg, 3 drawers, 83 tools) or the service case (13 kg, on wheels, 110 tools).

Finish and durability

The Max Edition comes in Nardo grey, black and red, or black, with a metallic lacquered paint. Whatever the maker, look at drawer alignment, paint consistency on the edges and runner play. In use, wipe off solvent and fuel splashes rather than letting them sit on the paint.

Budget: compare the whole set, not the drawer

Recommended retail prices incl. VAT across the range: Max Edition €899, Minibox €350, service case €199, workshop furniture from €1,250 (without tools). Rather than comparing price per drawer, compare the cost of the complete equipment you would otherwise assemble separately, allowing for the time spent putting it together.

Checklist before you buy

  • Do my longest tools fit in the drawers?
  • Does the set match my trade, or will I need to add to it?
  • Do all drawers lock together?
  • Are the wheels suited to my floor?
  • Will the trolley pass through every door on its route?
  • Who keeps the keys?
  • Does the budget cover the tools still missing?

Three profiles, three choices

The fixed workshop, every day

You work at the same station all day and take out plenty of tools: favour capacity, deep drawers and storage by family. The Max Edition answers this profile with its eight drawers.

The hangar or paddock

The trolley moves from machine to bench and several people use it. Wheels, central locking and self-locking drawers matter as much as the number of tools. Also think about where the trolley is parked and how much room it takes between jobs.

The travelling technician

Here weight decides. An 85 kg trolley has no place in a car boot: the service case or the Minibox are designed for that role, and the trolley stays in the workshop.

Looking after your trolley

  • Empty and dust the drawers from time to time: swarf and dust wear the runners.
  • Wipe tools before putting them away, especially after contact with a fluid.
  • Check that the wheels turn freely and nothing is wound around them.
  • Check the locks and keep a spare set of keys away from the trolley.

Take the time to check the exact drawer layout on the product page. A few minutes spent checking dimensions and contents against your own tools will save you from the most common regret: a trolley that is handsome but does not hold what you actually use.
